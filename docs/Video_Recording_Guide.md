# 🎬 KaratePose-AI — Video Recording Guide (A–Z)

**Purpose:** A complete, step-by-step field guide for recording all the video data
needed by the KaratePose-AI pipeline. Print this or keep it open on your phone
during the recording sessions.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The entire statistical analysis depends on these recordings being **identical
> in setup** across Set A (rashguard) and Set B (gi). Any difference in camera
> angle, distance, lighting, or framing between the two sets will invalidate
> the paired comparison. **Consistency is everything.**

## 1. Overview — What You're Recording

You are filming **3 practitioners × 3 techniques × 2 garment conditions × 10 reps**
= **180 individual technique repetitions**.

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| **Practitioners** | P01 (you, 1st Dan), P02 (Asian Championship winner), P03 (4th Dan Sensei) |
| **Garment Conditions** | **Set A** — Black compression rashguard + joint markers (control) |
| | **Set B** — 14oz heavyweight canvas Karate Gi (experimental) |
| **Techniques** | 1. *Oi-zuki* (lunge punch) |
| | 2. *Gedan-barai* (downward block) |
| | 3. *Zenkutsu-dachi → Kiba-dachi* (stance transition) |
| **Repetitions** | 10 consecutive reps per technique, per condition |
| **Camera** | iPhone 15 Pro Max — **4K 60fps** (primary) + **1080p 240fps** (validation) |

> **Total video files per practitioner:** 6 primary (3 techniques × 2 conditions)
> + 2 slow-motion validation = **8 files per person, 24 files total**.

## 3. Location Setup — The Room

### Requirements
| Parameter | Minimum Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Floor Space** | At least **6m × 4m** clear of obstacles |
| **Ceiling** | High enough that the tallest practitioner's fists don't get clipped at top of frame |
| **Lighting** | **500+ lux**, diffuse and uniform — avoid direct backlighting or harsh overhead spots |
| **Background** | Solid, matte, non-reflective wall (gray, dark blue, or plain white) |
| **Floor** | Non-slip surface (dojo mats, wooden sprung floor, or clean tile) |
| **Noise** | Minimal distractions — quiet enough for verbal cues |

### Setting Up the Space

```
                    ← 6m+ →
    ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
    │                                      │
    │     [CLEAN, NEUTRAL BACKGROUND]      │
    │                                      │
    │              ★ PRACTITIONER          │  ↕ 4m+
    │              (execution zone)        │
    │                                      │
    │     ─ ─ ─ ─ 4.5m ─ ─ ─ ─ ─          │
    │                                      │
    │          📷 CAMERA ON TRIPOD         │
    │                                      │
    └──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Step by step:**
1. Clear the area of any furniture, bags, water bottles — anything visible in frame.
2. Use your tape measure to mark **4.5 metres** from the background wall.
   Place a small piece of gaffer tape on the floor — this is where the tripod goes.
3. Place another tape mark at the practitioner's starting position.
4. Check for mirrors, windows, or shiny surfaces that might create reflections
   or variable lighting. Cover or avoid them.

## 4. Camera Setup — Hardware & Settings

### 4.1 Primary Recording Stream (The Important One)

Open the iPhone Camera app and set:

| Setting | Value | How to Set It |
|---|---|---|
| **Resolution** | 4K (3840×2160) | Settings → Camera → Record Video → 4K at 60fps |
| **Frame Rate** | 60 FPS | Same as above — select "4K at 60fps" |
| **HDR** | **OFF** | Settings → Camera → toggle OFF "HDR Video" |
| **Stabilisation** | **OFF** (if possible) | Use a tripod, you don't want software stabilisation cropping/warping the frame |
| **Focus & Exposure** | **AE/AF Lock** | Long-press on the practitioner in the viewfinder until "AE/AF LOCK" appears |
| **Grid** | ON | Settings → Camera → Grid → ON (helps with framing) |
| **Format** | Most Compatible (H.264) | Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible |

> [!WARNING]
> **HEVC vs H.264:** Set your iPhone to "Most Compatible" (H.264) format. The
> pipeline uses OpenCV which handles H.264 more reliably than HEVC/H.265. You
> can find this in Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible.

### 4.2 Slow-Motion Validation Stream (Secondary)

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| **Mode** | Slo-Mo |
| **Resolution** | 1080p (1920×1080) |
| **Frame Rate** | 240 FPS |
| **AE/AF Lock** | Yes — same as primary |

> You only need 240fps clips for **Oi-zuki** (the fastest technique) — not all 3 techniques.

### 4.3 Tripod & Framing

1. **Tripod height:** Set the phone camera lens at exactly **1.0 metre** from the floor.
   Measure this with your tape measure.
2. **Camera angle:** Phone should be perfectly **horizontal** (0° tilt). Use the
   built-in iPhone level (swipe to the ruler tool in the Measure app).
3. **Framing check:** Have someone stand at the execution position and do a full
   lunge punch (*oi-zuki*). Verify:
   - [ ] At least **0.5m of ground** visible below their feet
   - [ ] At least **0.5m of space** above their head/fist at peak extension
   - [ ] Their entire body stays in frame throughout the full movement
   - [ ] No body parts get cut off at the sides during forward stepping

> [!TIP]
> Do a quick test recording of 1 rep before the real session. Play it back and
> zoom in to check sharpness. If you see motion blur, the lighting is too dim.

## 5. Calibration Rod — Pixel-to-Meter Scaling

**Why:** The pipeline needs to convert pixel distances into real-world meters.
A 1.00m rod filmed at known distance gives us the conversion factor.

### Procedure
1. Before any practitioner steps in, place the **1.00m calibration rod**
   vertically at the exact spot where the practitioner will stand.
2. The rod should be:
   - Standing upright (or held by someone at the edges)
   - Clearly visible and fully in frame
   - At the same distance from the camera as the practitioner will be (4.5m)
3. Record a **5-second static clip** of just the rod. No movement needed.
4. Name this file: `CALIB_[DATE]_1m_rod.mov`
   - Example: `CALIB_20261215_1m_rod.mov`
5. Remove the rod before the actual technique recordings.

> [!NOTE]
> You only need **one calibration clip per session** (not per practitioner or
> per technique), as long as the camera doesn't move between takes.

## 6. Garment Protocols — Set A vs Set B

### 6.1 Set A — Control Condition (Rashguard + Markers)

This is your **ground truth proxy**. The tight clothing lets the AI see the body
clearly, and the markers give visual anchor points.

**Dressing procedure:**
1. Practitioner puts on the **matte black compression shirt** and **tights**.
2. Apply the **6 fluorescent markers** to the anatomical landmarks on the
   **camera-facing side** of the body:

```
        (4) Shoulder ●━━━━● (5) Elbow
                     |        \
                     |         ● (6) Wrist
                     |
          (3) Hip   ●
                     |
                     |
         (2) Knee   ●
                     |
                     |
        (1) Ankle   ●
```

**Landmark locations (feel for the bony bumps):**

| # | Marker | Anatomical Landmark | How to Find It |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ankle | Lateral Malleolus | The bony bump on the outside of the ankle |
| 2 | Knee | Lateral Femoral Condyle | The bony bump on the outside of the knee |
| 3 | Hip | Greater Trochanter | Stand on one leg, feel the bump at the top of the thigh bone |
| 4 | Shoulder | Acromion Process | The bony point at the very top/outside of the shoulder |
| 5 | Elbow | Lateral Epicondyle | The bony bump on the outside of the elbow |
| 6 | Wrist | Ulnar Styloid Process | The bony bump on the pinky side of the wrist |

> **Pro tip:** Press each marker firmly and smooth out air bubbles. If a marker
> falls off mid-recording, stop, reattach it, and redo that take.

### 6.2 Set B — Experimental Condition (14oz Gi)

**Dressing procedure:**
1. **Remove all fluorescent markers** from the body.
2. Practitioner puts on the 14oz Gi jacket, trousers, and belt.
3. Tie the obi in a standard square knot. Tuck the jacket overlap as you would
   in a normal dojo/competition setting.
4. **Do NOT pin or tighten the Gi** in any way that would reduce its natural
   looseness — we want maximum realistic occlusion.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Critical rule:** Do NOT wear the compression gear under the Gi for Set B.
> The markers would create bumps visible through the fabric, contaminating the
> experimental condition. Set B must be Gi-only (undergarments are fine).

## 7. The 3 Techniques — Execution Instructions

All techniques follow a **self-paced protocol**. Practitioners perform at
their natural cadence — no metronome or forced timing is required.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why self-paced?** Forced metronomic timing distorts natural karate
> biomechanics, particularly *Kime* (decisive finishing tension). Self-paced
> execution is more ecologically valid and is standard in published biomechanics
> research (Lees 2002, Quinzi et al. 2016). The pipeline's `rep_detector.py`
> module automatically segments reps using wrist/ankle velocity peaks.

```
Each rep cycle (self-paced):
    ┌─────────┐     ┌──────────┐     ┌─────────┐
    │ Ready   │ ──► │ Execute  │ ──► │ Reset   │ ──► (next rep)
    │ (Yoi)   │     │ (Strike) │     │ (Return)│
    └─────────┘     └──────────┘     └─────────┘
    Practitioner     Full technique    Step back to
    signals ready    at natural pace   start position
```

### Technique 1: *Oi-zuki* (Stepping Lunge Punch)

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 追い突き (*Oi-zuki*) |
| **Start** | Left *Zenkutsu-dachi* (front stance), left arm in *Gedan-barai* position |
| **Action** | Step forward with right leg into right *Zenkutsu-dachi*; right *Chudan Oi-zuki* (middle-level stepping punch) with full 180° forearm rotation |
| **End** | Hold final *Zenkutsu-dachi* + extended punch briefly (*Kime* finish) |
| **Reset** | Step back to starting position; brief natural pause; repeat |
| **Focus Joints** | Ankle, knee (sagittal velocity), hip translation, elbow extension |
| **Reps** | 10 × Set A, then 10 × Set B |

### Technique 2: *Gedan-barai* (Downward Block)

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 下段払い (*Gedan-barai*) |
| **Start** | Left *Zenkutsu-dachi*, right arm chambered at ear level |
| **Action** | Execute downward sweeping block with aggressive hip rotation (*Hanmi → Shomen*) |
| **End** | Hold final block position briefly (*Kime* finish) |
| **Reset** | Return to start; brief natural pause; repeat |
| **Focus Joints** | Torso/upper arm (severe canvas folding area), shoulder, wrist deceleration |
| **Reps** | 10 × Set A, then 10 × Set B |

### Technique 3: Stance Transition (*Zenkutsu-dachi → Kiba-dachi*)

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | 前屈立ち → 騎馬立ち |
| **Start** | Right *Zenkutsu-dachi* (deep front stance, ~60% weight forward) |
| **Action** | 90° pivot into *Kiba-dachi* (horse-riding stance, 50/50 weight distribution, wide knee abduction) |
| **End** | Hold *Kiba-dachi* briefly (*Kime* finish) |
| **Reset** | Pivot back to *Zenkutsu-dachi*; brief natural pause; repeat |
| **Focus Joints** | Hip occlusion by trouser folds, CoG vertical/lateral shift |
| **Reps** | 10 × Set A, then 10 × Set B |

> [!TIP]
> **Coaching cue for consistency:** Tell each practitioner: "Imagine you're
> doing a grading. Every rep should look identical. Same depth, same speed,
> same *kime*. If you make a mistake or lose balance, we'll discard that rep
> and add an extra one at the end."

## 8. Recording Procedure — Per-Take Workflow

Follow this **exact sequence** for every single take:

### Before You Press Record
1. ✅ Confirm camera hasn't moved (check the tripod tape mark).
2. ✅ Confirm AE/AF Lock is still active (long-press the screen if unsure).
3. ✅ Confirm practitioner is in the correct garment condition.

### The Recording Sequence

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Step 1: 🔴 Press RECORD                                      │
│                                                                │
│  Step 2: 👏 SYNC CLAP — Practitioner claps hands sharply       │
│          in frame (creates visual + audio sync point)          │
│                                                                │
│  Step 3: 🧍 STATIC POSE — Hold Yoi or T-pose for 3 seconds   │
│          (this gives the AI a clean starting reference)        │
│                                                                │
│  Step 4: 🥋 PERFORM 10 REPS — Execute the technique           │
│          at natural pace with brief pauses between reps        │
│          (rep_detector.py auto-segments afterwards)             │
│                                                                │
│  Step 5: 🧍 FINAL HOLD — 3-second static hold after last rep  │
│                                                                │
│  Step 6: ⏹️ STOP RECORD                                       │
│                                                                │
│  Step 7: 📋 LOG IT — Write the take details in the data       │
│          collection sheet (file name, condition, notes)        │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Estimated Duration Per Take
- 3s (static) + 10 reps at natural pace + 3s (final hold) ≈ **60–120 seconds** (varies with natural cadence)

> [!NOTE]
> **If something goes wrong mid-take** (marker falls off, practitioner slips,
> phone notification pops up, someone walks through frame): Stop recording,
> note it in the log sheet as "VOID", and redo the entire take from Step 1.
> Do NOT try to salvage half a recording.

## 9. Full Session Schedule — The Master Timetable

Here is the recommended order for a **single practitioner session**. Expect
each session to take approximately **60–90 minutes** including setup and
outfit changes.

### Phase 0: Setup (15 min)
- [ ] Set up tripod, measure 4.5m distance, confirm 1.0m height
- [ ] Check lighting (500+ lux)
- [ ] Record the calibration rod clip
- [ ] Test recording — 1 rep of oi-zuki, play back, check framing

### Phase 1: Set A — Control / Rashguard + Markers (25 min)

| Order | Take | Technique | Reps | File Name |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | T01 | Oi-zuki | 10 | `SETA_OIZUKI_T01_60FPS.mov` |
| 2 | T02 | Gedan-barai | 10 | `SETA_GEDAN_T01_60FPS.mov` |
| 3 | T03 | Stance Transition | 10 | `SETA_STANCE_T01_60FPS.mov` |

### ☕ Break + Costume Change (10 min)
- Remove all fluorescent markers
- Change into 14oz Gi
- **Do NOT move the camera or tripod!**

### Phase 2: Set B — Experimental / Gi (25 min)

| Order | Take | Technique | Reps | File Name |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | T04 | Oi-zuki | 10 | `SETB_OIZUKI_T01_60FPS.mov` |
| 5 | T05 | Gedan-barai | 10 | `SETB_GEDAN_T01_60FPS.mov` |
| 6 | T06 | Stance Transition | 10 | `SETB_STANCE_T01_60FPS.mov` |

### Phase 3: Slow-Motion Validation (10 min)
> Only for Oi-zuki — the fastest technique.

| Order | Take | Technique | Reps | File Name |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | T07 | Oi-zuki (Slo-Mo, Set A) | 5 | `SETA_OIZUKI_T02_240FPS.mov` |
| 8 | T08 | Oi-zuki (Slo-Mo, Set B) | 5 | `SETB_OIZUKI_T02_240FPS.mov` |

### 📊 Repeat for Each Practitioner

| Session | Practitioner | Suggested Day |
|---|---|---|
| S01 | P01 (You) | Day 1 |
| S02 | P02 (Asian Champ) | Day 2 |
| S03 | P03 (4th Dan Sensei) | Day 3 |

> [!TIP]
> **Randomise the order** of Set A and Set B across practitioners to avoid
> fatigue bias. For P02, start with Set B first (Gi), then do Set A
> (rashguard). This way, if a practitioner gets tired, the fatigue effect
> is distributed equally across both conditions.

## 10. File Naming & Transfer to Mac

### 10.1 File Naming Convention

```
[SET]_[TECHNIQUE]_[TAKE_ID]_[FPS]FPS.mov
```

| Component | Values |
|---|---|
| `SET` | `SETA` (control/rashguard) or `SETB` (experimental/gi) |
| `TECHNIQUE` | `OIZUKI`, `GEDAN`, or `STANCE` |
| `TAKE_ID` | `T01` (primary 60fps), `T02` (slow-mo 240fps) |
| `FPS` | `60` or `240` |

**Examples:**
- `SETA_OIZUKI_T01_60FPS.mov`
- `SETB_GEDAN_T01_60FPS.mov`
- `SETA_OIZUKI_T02_240FPS.mov`

### 10.2 Folder Structure on Your Mac

The pipeline expects this exact structure inside your project:

```
Karate Pose AI/
└── data/
    └── raw/
        ├── calibration/
        │   └── CALIB_20261215_1m_rod.mov
        │
        ├── set_a_control/
        │   ├── P01/
        │   │   ├── SETA_OIZUKI_T01_60FPS.mov
        │   │   ├── SETA_GEDAN_T01_60FPS.mov
        │   │   └── SETA_STANCE_T01_60FPS.mov
        │   ├── P02/
        │   │   └── ...
        │   └── P03/
        │       └── ...
        │
        └── set_b_experimental/
            ├── P01/
            │   ├── SETB_OIZUKI_T01_60FPS.mov
            │   ├── SETB_GEDAN_T01_60FPS.mov
            │   └── SETB_STANCE_T01_60FPS.mov
            ├── P02/
            │   └── ...
            └── P03/
                └── ...
```

> This matches the `PATHS` dictionary in `src/config.py`, where:
> - `PATHS["set_a"]` → `data/raw/set_a_control/`
> - `PATHS["set_b"]` → `data/raw/set_b_experimental/`
> - `PATHS["calibration"]` → `data/raw/calibration/`

### 10.3 How to Transfer Files from iPhone → Mac

**Option A — AirDrop (Fastest)**
1. Select all videos from a session in the Photos app
2. Tap Share → AirDrop → Your Mac
3. Rename files to match the convention above
4. Move into the correct project folders

**Option B — Cable Transfer**
1. Connect iPhone via USB-C/Lightning cable
2. Open Image Capture (built-in macOS app)
3. Select videos, download to a temp folder
4. Rename and organise

**Option C — iCloud (Slowest, but automatic)**
1. If iCloud Photos is enabled, videos sync automatically
2. Download from Photos on Mac, rename, and move

> [!WARNING]
> **Rename files BEFORE placing them in the project folder.** If you put
> `IMG_1234.MOV` into `set_a_control/`, you will lose track of what is what
> very quickly. Always rename first.

## 11. Post-Session Verification Checklist

Run through this **after each practitioner's session**, before they leave:

### Video Quality Check
- [ ] Open **every** recorded file on the iPhone and scrub through it
- [ ] Confirm all 10 reps are visible and complete (no cut-offs)
- [ ] Confirm the sync clap is visible at the start
- [ ] Confirm no motion blur (watch the fastest frame of oi-zuki closely)
- [ ] Confirm the practitioner's full body stays in frame throughout
- [ ] Confirm the background is clean (no one walked through)

### Marker Check (Set A only)
- [ ] Confirm all 6 markers are visible in the footage throughout all 10 reps
- [ ] If a marker fell off during recording, note which rep and consider a retake

### Data Sheet Check

- [ ] Notes column filled with any issues (e.g., "marker re-stuck after rep 4")

### File Count
- [ ] 6 primary videos (3 techniques × 2 conditions) ✓
- [ ] 2 slow-motion validation videos ✓
- [ ] 1 calibration rod video (shared across all practitioners) ✓

## 12. Troubleshooting & Common Mistakes

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| **Motion blur on fast punches** | Lighting is too dim. Add more lights or move to a brighter room. The iPhone needs 500+ lux to maintain 1/500s shutter speed at 60fps. |
| **Practitioner goes out of frame** | Move the camera back (increase distance to 5m) or switch to the wide-angle lens (0.5× on iPhone). Re-record the calibration rod at the new distance. |
| **AE/AF Lock keeps resetting** | This happens if you switch apps or lock the phone. Re-lock focus/exposure before every single take. |
| **Fluorescent markers not visible** | Use brighter/larger markers, or add a desk lamp pointing at the practitioner. The markers need contrast against the black compression gear. |
| **iPhone runs out of storage** | 4K 60fps ≈ 400 MB/minute. Each take is 60–120 seconds ≈ 400–800 MB. Budget **~5 GB per practitioner** (8 takes). Transfer files to Mac between sessions. |
| **Tripod gets bumped between Set A and Set B** | If you can't get it back to the exact position, you must redo the calibration rod clip. Ideally, tape the tripod legs to the floor. |
| **Practitioner fatigues and reps slow down** | Give a 5-minute break between techniques. Offer water. Quality > speed. |
| **The Gi belt comes undone mid-take** | Re-tie and redo the take. Mark the voided take in your log. |
| **iPhone overheats during extended 4K recording** | Take the phone case off. Record in a cool room. If it overheats, wait 5 minutes before the next take. |
| **Uneven lighting (half the body in shadow)** | Move to a spot with uniform overhead lighting. Avoid filming next to windows where one side gets sunlight. |
