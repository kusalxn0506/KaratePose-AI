2. Visualization Engine Validation (Synthetic Data Proof-of-Concept)

To ensure the pipeline is ready for immediate analysis post-data collection, the visualization and statistical modules were validated using synthetic data sets. The following figures demonstrate the system's capability to automatically generate publication-ready graphics and statistical proofs once real experimental data is ingested.

Figure 1: Statistical Summary Table

Figure 1: Automated statistical summary applying Wilcoxon signed-rank tests to evaluate tracking degradation between baseline (Control) and experimental (Karate Gi) conditions.
Demonstrates: Whether the drop in tracking accuracy caused by the Karate Gi is statistically significant (p < 0.05), proving the necessity for a secondary filtering algorithm.

Figure 2: Spatial Jitter Comparison (All Joints)

Figure 2: Visual comparison of median spatial jitter across all monitored joints, quantifying the occlusion interference caused by rigid fabric.
Demonstrates: That the AI struggles most with distal joints (wrists and ankles) when a Gi is worn, indicated by the taller boxplots which represent higher tracking instability.

Figure 3: Single Joint Jitter Isolation

Figure 3: Isolated comparative analysis of spatial jitter for a specific distal joint (wrist), highlighting tracking instability induced by baggy sleeves.
Demonstrates: The exact spread and severity of the AI's coordinate tracking error caused by the "bagging" effect of the Gi sleeve during the extension of a punch.

Figure 4: Tracking Confidence Heatmap

Figure 4: Heatmap tracking frame-by-frame pose estimation confidence, allowing the system to isolate specific occlusion events during high-velocity kinematics.
Demonstrates: Exactly when the AI goes "blind" (darker vertical bands), proving that rigid fabric causes simultaneous multi-joint tracking failure during the high-velocity snapping phase of a technique.

Figure 5: Tracking Confidence Timeseries

Figure 5: Timeseries analysis identifying acute confidence drops, simulating a fabric occlusion event during technique execution.
Demonstrates: The rapid degradation and recovery of the AI's confidence, simulating how a fabric fold temporarily hides the limb from the camera lens.

Figure 6: Spatial Jitter Timeseries

Figure 6: Frame-by-frame tracking of spatial jitter, serving as a primary anomaly detection mechanism for sudden pose estimation failures.
Demonstrates: The volatility of the predicted joint coordinate over time. Massive spikes indicate the exact frames where the AI lost the anatomical joint and mistakenly locked onto the fabric instead.
